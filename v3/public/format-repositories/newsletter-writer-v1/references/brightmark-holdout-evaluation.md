# Brightmark blind holdout evaluation

The agent received Brightmark newsletters 1 and 2, the website facts, and the
onboarding-kit brief. Newsletter 3 stayed hidden until after generation.

## Hidden newsletter behavior

The held-out newsletter opens on a laptop, login, and plain box of loose cables.
It reframes the box as part of the welcome, argues for five useful pieces rather
than twenty products, explains assembly and direct shipping, and closes by
asking for headcount and start date.

## Blind output behavior

The generated newsletter opens on loose equipment in a box, treats packaging as
part of the welcome, gives each product a practical job, resists filling the box,
explains assembly and direct shipping, and closes by asking for headcount and
start date.

## Result

| Behavior | Held-out sample | Blind output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete opening | Laptop, login, plain box, loose cables | Onboarding kit, loose equipment, box |
| Central reframe | The box is part of the welcome | Packaging does some of the welcoming |
| Selection rule | The right five, not twenty products | Give every item a job; keep the mix focused |
| Operations | Assemble and ship before day one | Assemble and ship to individual recipients |
| CTA shape | Headcount and start date | Headcount and start date |
| Tone | Plain, practical, no hype | Plain, practical, no hype |

The blind output reproduced the important writing behavior without copying the
hidden text. This is qualitative evidence from a three-email fixture, not a
claim of author identification or a universal style score.

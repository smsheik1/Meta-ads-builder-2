# 3D Breakdown Ecommerce Benchmark

This benchmark defines the creative quality bar for Wiggly Style B. It is derived from the supplied Franky Shaw / BioRoot ecommerce reference and compares the current Gruns v2 export as the baseline to beat.

This is not a request to reproduce another creator shot-for-shot. It captures the reusable production system: a concrete false assumption, a hidden obstacle, a physical mechanism demonstration, grounded proof, and a direct product payoff.

## Gold Reference Story Map

| Time | Narration job | Visual job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-4s | Ordinary action plus false assumption: swallow a capsule and assume absorption. | Silent recurring demonstrator handles the capsule; the body route becomes visible. |
| 4-11s | Hidden obstacle: curcumin is difficult for the body to use. | Curcumin physically fails to cross a cellular/body barrier. |
| 11-17s | First mechanism: black pepper/piperine increases bioavailability. | The demonstrator adds the missing component and the blocked path changes state. |
| 17-26s | Second false assumption: a large powder pile can look powerful. | Exaggerated scale comparison exposes bulk powder versus concentrated active material. |
| 26-34s | Product proof: standardized concentrated extract. | Powder is compressed/refined into a concentrated product component. |
| 34-45s | Delivery obstacle: curcumin clumps in water and is harder to transport. | Particles visibly clump, stick, and stall inside a clear delivery system. |
| 45-50s | Delivery mechanism: MCT oil keeps it dispersed. | Oil changes particle behavior and the transport path begins moving again. |
| 50-57s | Supporting formula: ginger, vitamin C, and bee propolis join. | Each ingredient enters the same physical system instead of starting a new visual world. |
| 57-75s | Product payoff: joint, inflammatory-response, and antioxidant support. | The demonstrator moves through control-room and chain-reaction metaphors that make invisible processes visible. |
| 75-88s | Comparison: loose turmeric powder versus coordinated formula. | Two capsules/formulas are physically compared; components lock into one coordinated system. |
| 88-99s | Product reframe and CTA. | Accurate product pack, compact benefit summary, and direct product action close the story. |

## What Actually Creates The Effect

1. **Causal density:** nearly every sentence causes a visible object or system to change.
2. **One accumulating explanation:** each mechanism solves the obstacle introduced immediately before it.
3. **Silent demonstrator continuity:** the recurring human is a scale proxy and product demonstrator, never the narrator or host.
4. **Physicalized invisible processes:** absorption, concentration, dispersion, and chain reactions become objects, barriers, pipes, particles, and machines.
5. **Stable visual grammar:** the blue technical world, demonstrator identity, product relationship, and camera language persist while scale and setting vary.
6. **Product delayed, not forgotten:** the story earns the packshot by teaching the mechanism first, then returns to the accurate product.
7. **Kinetic captions are overlays:** generated pixels do not carry readable copy; captions support narration during assembly.
8. **Direct close:** the ending tells the buyer what product to get or try. It does not end on an abstract mechanism slogan.

The reference is not strong merely because it uses a blue grid or 3D character. A blue background with repeated anatomy tunnels still fails.

## 20-Second Wiggly Adaptation

Wiggly compresses the same causal arc rather than copying the 99-second shot count.

| Asset | Locked job |
| --- | --- |
| Script beat 1 | Concrete use/arrival plus false assumption. |
| Script beat 2 | Hidden obstacle or consequence. |
| Script beat 3 | Product mechanism begins changing the obstacle. |
| Script beat 4 | Evidence-grounded reveal and payoff. |
| Script beat 5 | Product reframe plus direct buyer-action CTA. |
| Storyboard frame 1 | Ordinary product-use action; category is visually clear. |
| Storyboard frame 2 | The hidden obstacle becomes physically visible. |
| Storyboard frame 3 | Product mechanism setup; demonstrator/product relationship persists. |
| Storyboard frame 4 | Peak impossible reveal that teaches new information. |
| Storyboard frame 5 | Selected evidence becomes a visible payoff. |
| Storyboard frame 6 | Accurate product and direct CTA-safe final state. |
| Clip 1 | Frames 1-3: assumption, obstacle, mechanism setup. |
| Clip 2 | Frames 4-6: reveal, proof/payoff, product close. |

Every frame transition must introduce a new object state, scale, spatial relationship, or causal result. Consecutive near-duplicate tunnel, anatomy, presenter, or packshot views fail even when technically attractive.

## Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension `0`, `1`, or `2`. A candidate passes at `16/20` or higher with no hard failure.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete hook | Abstract claim or brand intro. | Concrete but low-tension use moment. | Immediate product action plus a surprising false assumption or consequence. |
| Causal mechanism | Features or credentials are listed. | Obstacle and mechanism are adjacent but weakly connected. | Each mechanism visibly changes the obstacle introduced before it. |
| Narration-to-visual mapping | Lines cannot be drawn. | Some lines have generic illustrative footage. | Every line owns a specific object, action, transformation, or result. |
| State-change cadence | Repeated/static shots create dead air. | Some meaningful changes with repetition. | A meaningful visual state change lands every 1.5-3 seconds. |
| Silent demonstrator continuity | Missing, inconsistent, or speaking/lipsyncing. | Appears but mostly poses beside product. | Recurs as scale proxy and physically demonstrates the mechanism without speaking. |
| World continuity | Unrelated styles/environments reset the story. | Shared palette but inconsistent camera/object logic. | One coherent world persists across studio, cutaway, and mechanism scales. |
| Evidence grounding | Unsupported or decorative proof. | Evidence is spoken/shown but not causal. | Selected evidence is the spine and visibly explains the payoff. |
| Product identity | Product disappears or wrong/generic packaging appears. | Product category is right but grounding is weak. | Real product shape/reference persists and returns accurately at payoff. |
| Clean generated media | Baked text, logos, UI, narrator lipsync, or double VO. | Minor artifacts or ambiguous mouth motion. | No readable generated text; silent demonstrator; one unseen narrator. |
| Buyer payoff and CTA | Abstract slogan or no action. | Product is named but action/payoff is weak. | Product is reframed for the buyer and the CTA names a clear next action. |

### Hard Failures

- Wrong product, merch stand-in, or invented packaging.
- Unsupported medical, performance, financial, comparative, or measurable claim.
- Demonstrator visibly speaks or lip-syncs the unseen narration.
- Readable generated captions, labels, logos, UI, or pseudo-text inside image/video pixels.
- Product/evidence lock is lost between selected card, script, storyboard, anchors, or clips.
- Ending has no direct buyer action.

## Evaluation Set

### Development fixtures

1. **Supplement:** Seed DS-01. Tests capsule journey, digestive obstacle, capsule-in-capsule mechanism, regulated claim restraint, and product accuracy.
2. **Commodity/gifting:** David's Cookies. Tests emotional/customer tension without inventing freshness engineering or shipping physics.
3. **Physical gadget:** a one-hand jar opener with evidence-backed gripping teeth and self-adjusting ring. Tests visible mechanical causality without anatomy/science defaults.

### Holdouts

1. **Beauty refill product:** tests product layers, refill mechanism, and beauty/health risk restraint.
2. **Pet water fountain:** tests process pipeline/filter mechanism and product continuity without a human-body metaphor.

Development examples may teach structure, but holdout brand wording, objects, and mechanisms must not appear in the prompt examples.

## Acceptance Gates

1. Text/script plans pass the rubric offline across all development fixtures.
2. Holdouts pass without copying development nouns, products, or reveal details.
3. `/create` mocked Playwright flow preserves the selected direction and exact evidence through script, six-frame storyboard, two anchors, and two clip plans.
4. Human approves the paid storyboard before anchors.
5. Human approves both anchors before clips.
6. One paid acceptance run is capped at five Replicate calls with no automatic retry.
7. Final 20-second MP4 scores at least `16/20`, has no hard failure, and clearly beats Gruns v2.

# Talking Fish News: Editorial Desk

## Job

Turn real brand evidence into a reportable event before anyone writes a script or draws a frame.

The desk is not a copywriter. It decides whether a business has something that can honestly be
framed as a short report. If it does not, the format should stop here.

## Inputs

- Website or a short brand brief.
- One objective: product, company story, customer problem, or custom topic.
- An evidence inventory: product photos, founder/history proof, real customer language,
  product mechanism, comparison, measurable claim, and official screens.

## The Newsworthy Event Test

An idea passes only when all four are true:

1. **Specific:** A viewer can repeat the event in one sentence.
2. **Verifiable:** At least one official asset or source supports it.
3. **Visual:** At least two pieces of proof can be shown without tiny text.
4. **Consequential:** The event changes what a buyer thinks they should do, avoid, or notice.

Reject generic benefits such as "better quality," "trusted service," "smart AI," or "great
support." They are ads, not reports.

## Five Concept Lanes

Generate one premise in each lane, then choose the strongest evidence-led one.

| Lane | Event grammar | Good evidence | Bad version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hidden cost | "[Common habit] is quietly causing [concrete loss]." | Price comparison, waste, process proof | "Our product saves money." |
| Category change | "[Old default] has been replaced by [new mechanism]." | Before/after, product cutaway, real workflow | "We are innovative." |
| Evidence alert | "New proof shows [specific fact] about [problem]." | Test, case study, primary result | "Experts love us." |
| Customer incident | "[Person or team] was about to [lose/miss/fix] [thing] until [mechanism]." | Supported friction, product interaction, outcome | "A customer loves us." |
| Origin report | "The unlikely decision behind [brand] still changes [result]." | Founder/history evidence, old photos, present-day proof | "Our origin story is inspiring." |

## Concept Slate Contract

Each concept card must return:

- `news_event`: 8-14 plain words, no brand name required.
- `why_it_matters`: one buyer consequence.
- `proof_assets`: two to four large, recognizable visual proofs.
- `brand_role`: the factual mechanism that belongs in the report.
- `risk`: unsupported / too-small-to-read / not-newsworthy.
- `reportability`: pass or fail.

The desk shows five cards only when at least two pass. Otherwise it explains that this format
needs a proof-heavy story and asks for a different objective or source page.

## Anti-Ad Gate

Reject any card that:

- Starts with the company name or a product feature.
- Needs the viewer to read a small UI panel, legal copy, or screenshot to understand it.
- Has no visible change between the first and last proof image.
- Uses `breaking`, `shocking`, `new study`, `record-breaking`, or a numeric result without
  exact support.
- Ends in "shop now," "book a call," or a feature list.

## What The User Sees

The agent asks one question only if it cannot pick an objective from the source:

`What should this report be about: one product, your brand story, a customer problem, or your own idea?`

Everything else is defaulted. Before any paid work, the user chooses one concept and sees:

- the event;
- the proof it will use;
- the 15-20 second report outline;
- a cost estimate for the next paid step.
